        <div class="subsection-title">Key swing variables for the equity</div>
        <p>The first swing variable is hyperscaler and frontier-lab capex allocation between merchant GPUs and increasingly prevalent custom accelerators from partners like Broadcom and AMD, with recent reports suggesting AVGO’s AI compute and networking share could roughly double by 2027 while AMD’s MI355X/MI400 roadmap targets lower tokens-per-dollar versus NVIDIA.[11][24] The second is the durability of hyperscaler AI capex growth beyond the 2024–2026 training super-cycle as inference workloads scale and enterprise adoption broadens, a thesis supported by AMD’s updated projection that the data center AI accelerator market could exceed $500–600B by 2028 at a 60%+ CAGR.[4] The third is regulatory and export-control friction, with recent limitations on China-bound accelerators already costing NVDA roughly $8B in H20 revenue and creating forecast risk around specific SKUs and regions.[2][21] How these factors resolve will determine whether NVDA’s multiple compresses toward mature megacaps or re-expands toward premium software-like valuations as investors gain confidence in a longer AI infrastructure runway.[30][33]</p>

            <p>1) Prob: High × Impact: High — AI capex normalization and digestion. If hyperscaler and large-cloud capex growth fell below 10% annually for a sustained period, NVIDIA’s data center revenue could undershoot consensus by 20–30% and compress the EV/Revenue multiple into single digits, implying 20–40% downside from current levels.[4][9]</p>
            <p>2) Prob: Medium × Impact: High — Custom ASIC and in-house accelerator substitution. A scenario where Broadcom, AMD, and in-house ASICs capture 20–30% of workloads currently running on NVIDIA GPUs by 2028 could cap revenue growth near 20–25% and force more aggressive pricing, significantly eroding margin and the premium multiple.[4][11][24]</p>
            <p>3) Prob: Medium × Impact: Medium–High — Regulatory and export-control expansion. If export regimes broadened to encompass a wider set of AI accelerators and regions, revenue at risk could exceed the ~$8B hit previously seen on H20, potentially reducing total revenue by more than 10% and constraining growth visibility.[2][21]</p>
            <p>4) Prob: Low–Medium × Impact: High — Supply-chain disruption in HBM and advanced packaging. An extended disruption at key suppliers like TSMC, SK Hynix, or Micron or geopolitical frictions in Taiwan could sharply constrain shipments and delay ramps such as Blackwell and Rubin, hitting both revenue and NVIDIA’s reputation for flawless execution.[10]</p>
            <p>5) Prob: Low × Impact: Medium — Moat erosion in software and tools. If open-source or alternative acceleration stacks were to erode CUDA’s dominance and make workloads more portable, hyperscalers could arbitrage silicon vendors more aggressively, reining in NVIDIA’s pricing power and commoditizing parts of the stack.[11][23]</p>

            <div class="subsection-title">Bull Case</div>
            <p>1) AI accelerator TAM upside and inference wave — The AMD and BofA frameworks may prove conservative if inference-centric workloads like agents and reasoning models proliferate, pushing TAM well above $600B by 2028 and absorbing both NVIDIA’s and competitors’ capacity while preserving robust pricing power.[4][30]</p>
            <p>2) Platform lock-in slows ASIC share gains — CUDA, cuDNN, and vertical stacks such as NVIDIA AI Enterprise make it operationally and financially painful for enterprises to migrate away from NVIDIA, which could limit ASIC penetration to the most extreme hyperscaler workloads and leave the broader market firmly in NVIDIA’s orbit.[11][23]</p>
            <p>3) System-level innovation extends lead — Rubin supercomputer platforms promising significant reductions in GPUs required for training and 10× higher inference throughput may enable NVIDIA to deliver better total cost of ownership than custom ASICs, especially when customers value flexibility and time-to-solution over absolute efficiency.[5][18]</p>
