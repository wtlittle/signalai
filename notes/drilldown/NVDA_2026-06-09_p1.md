        <h2>5. Business Model and KPI Dashboard</h2>
        <h3>Business Model Overview</h3>
        <p>
          NVIDIA’s business model is centered on selling high-performance computing platforms—primarily GPUs and associated hardware and software stacks—that enable accelerated computing for AI, graphics, and high-performance computing workloads. Revenue is generated through the sale of datacenter products, client and gaming GPUs, professional visualization hardware, and automotive and edge-computing solutions, with the datacenter segment now constituting the dominant growth and profit engine. The company captures value not only through its silicon but through a vertically integrated ecosystem of system-level designs, networking products, and software layers such as CUDA and domain-specific SDKs that create switching costs and drive repeat purchases as workloads scale. While the revenue model is predominantly transactional hardware with lumpy capex cycles, NVIDIA increasingly embeds software, libraries and cloud-delivered services to introduce more recurring and usage-linked components over time. The combination of hardware performance leadership and software ecosystem lock-in allows the company to price at a premium and sustain unusually high gross margins in what is nominally a semiconductor business.
        </p>
