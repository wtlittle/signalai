In response to customer concentration, bulls argue that hyperscaler dependence cuts both ways: cloud providers have built their AI roadmaps, software stacks, and toolchains around NVIDIA hardware and CUDA, making large‑scale substitution costly and risky, particularly in training where model performance and time‑to‑market are critical.[19] Rather than replacing NVIDIA, custom silicon and AMD hardware are more likely to coexist as secondary options, enabling price negotiations at the margin but not dislodging NVIDIA from most mission‑critical workloads. Bulls also note that even if NVIDIA’s AI accelerator market share falls from ~80% to 60–70% by 2028, as some market models suggest, the total addressable market is expected to expand from about $200&nbsp;billion to over $500&nbsp;billion, so NVIDIA’s absolute revenue could still grow substantially.[10][2]
