Three structural drivers underpin this growth: the shift from general-purpose to accelerated computing for AI workloads, the exponential increase in model parameter counts requiring more compute, and the transition from training-focused to inference-heavy AI deployments that demand different hardware architectures. NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang has stated the company sees "at least $1 trillion" in revenue opportunity from AI chips through 2027, reflecting the massive scale of the infrastructure build-out required for enterprise and sovereign AI adoption. This growth is occurring against a backdrop of significant supply chain constraints, with TSMC recently warning NVIDIA and Broadcom of capacity squeezes as AI chip demand surges beyond manufacturing capabilities[48].</p>
